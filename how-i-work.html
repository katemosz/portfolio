How I work

The method behind the case studies

The case studies show what happened. This page is the how — how I scope a study, write the script, document as I go, and turn sessions into decisions the team can act on.

Research isn't a phase

When I joined my team, research wasn't part of how anyone worked. I built that from the ground up. Today every initiative starts with research, product owners run their own discovery interviews, and Data Science tracks how features are actually used as a matter of habit.

I've run studies with planners on nearly every continent except Africa, across very different levels of experience — from junior planners to senior strategists — because the same feature lands differently depending on who's holding it.

Scoping a study

Before I write a single question I get clear on the decision the research has to serve — what changes depending on the answer. A study that can't change a decision isn't worth running.

Documenting as I go

Everything lives in one place the whole team can reach — the plan, the raw notes, the synthesis, and the decisions that came out of it. A year later, anyone can see not just what we decided but why.

From sessions to decisions

I cluster raw feedback by topic, separate a fixable complaint from a real structural preference, and land on must / should / could — so the readout hands the team a prioritised list, not a transcript.

AI in the workflow

I work with AI tools daily — Figma Make, Lovable, Claude — as a thinking partner: pressure-testing ideas, drafting stimuli, and speeding up synthesis. I always start from my own sketches, and the final call is always mine.

I've also built small research agents to stress-test concepts before a session, and I co-lead an in-progress AI research-agent project with Data Science — where I've learned what LLMs do well, where they fail confidently, and the guardrails a product has to put around them.
