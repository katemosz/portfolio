WPP Media · Full platform redesign · 2023–2025

Rebuilding Campaign Planning

One agency’s internal tool became the company’s platform — and 2× faster to use.

A full redesign of the app media planners use to build campaigns. My team was the first trusted to pilot WPP’s new company-wide design system — so the rebuild had to prove the system could carry a real, complex product.

Role
Lead Product Designer
Team
Designers, Product Owner, Engineering
Timeframe
~12 months
(late 2023 – early 2025)
Company
WPP Media

One media agency’s internal tool. WPP wanted it to be the campaign-planning platform for the whole company — and picked our team to go first, ahead of everyone, on a brand-new design system.

The brief could have been a component swap. I set the bar higher: fix the real pain points, simplify the flow, make it fast, and add smart defaults wherever the data justified them.

What changed

Drag each slider. Same task, before and after the redesign.

Navigation

A dense, multi-level tab bar became one guided path.

Setup used to sprawl across nested tabs you had to hold in your head. Now it’s a single left-hand stepper, in the order a planner actually works.

Goal setting

Touchpoints

Before After

A complicated, multi-element navigation became one simplified path — cutting cognitive load and making the whole setup flow 2× faster, through merged settings, smart defaults, and a backend rebuilt to load only what’s needed.

A year-long rebuild, on a paper trail.

Not just good instincts — data, an audit with engineering, user validation, and a defined success bar. The short version — scroll:

01 · Data

Grounded it in data

Backend usage, past research, and support’s most common complaints — before touching a screen.

02 · Audit

Audited the entire flow

Mapped every step, then split with engineering what had to change from what could wait.

03 · Redesign

Redesigned the flow

Wireframed a reorganised path that merged related settings into smarter steps, then built it up in fidelity.

04 · Validate

Validated with users

Ran the interviews and prototype tests myself, and turned findings into concrete direction.

05 · Handover

Handed over & built

A full handover package, then stayed close to engineering through the build.

06 · Measure

Defined success & kept listening

Set the success bar before calling it done, walked users through the changes, kept the loop open after launch.

Retention turned out to be seasonal — heaviest at the start, middle, and end of the year — something a redesign alone was never going to move. I show the number; I don’t dress it up.

“This is so much easier to follow — every action finally feels like it’s in the right place.” — media planner, usability session

What changed for the business

Campaign setup got 2× faster.

A re-sequenced flow, data-informed defaults, and a backend that loads only what’s needed — together, not any one fix.

Fewer clicks, no lost place mid-setup.

The option people reached for most right after recalculating got its own step, in the right spot in the flow.

Advanced settings stopped taxing everyone else.

Options less-experienced planners never touched moved to a side panel or were marked optional — progressive disclosure, not everything on by default.

It became the reference other teams build from.

As the company rolled out its new design system, our rebuild was the implementation teams looked to — the payoff for going first.
