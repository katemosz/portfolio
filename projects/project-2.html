WPP Media · 5 shipped fixes · 2024–2026

A run of smaller problems

Five things that were quietly broken — and how I fixed them.

Shorter pieces of work across a media-planning platform. A validation error nobody could read. Benchmark data locked behind a file upload. A review screen people dreaded. Each one below is a problem, a fix, and what the business got.

Small surface, real stakes. Each of these was a support-ticket generator, a blocked workflow, or code engineering couldn’t safely maintain.

01
Touchpoints · AI

AI reads the rejection.

RF-curve files were rejected with one red sentence and no pointer to which of dozens of cells was wrong — the module’s most common support ticket. With backend, I defined an AI reply that names what’s wrong, why, the exact cells, and a correct example — one error type at a time, capped so it never becomes a wall of red.

Outcome
Zero RF-curve support tickets since launch (March 2026) — down from the single most frequent escalation in the module.
Skill gained
Structuring an AI’s answers — a fixed response template, token and model limits, and a real feel for where an LLM’s limits sit.
02
Touchpoints · Data Science

A benchmark ROI library.

To see ROI curves you had to upload your own econometric model — which almost no team has. I ran the discovery that unblocked it (~10 interviews, five regions), then shaped the flow: pick a few familiar attributes; curves auto-match to your touchpoints, editable per row. No data science request, no file, no one-to-one client data.

Outcome
In production with one of the firm’s largest clients, whose planners add library curves to live plans. Econometrician sign-off after nine months of cross-market modelling.
Skill gained
A seat in the senior, business-side roadmap discussions a PO usually owns — and a closer partnership with Data Science.
03
Campaigns · Budget optimisation

Budget limits, backed by history.

Constraints were manual entry, one row per channel — from a client, an investment team, or a hunch. I added a two-tab panel: your team’s standing limits, and smart benchmarks Data Science built from thousands of past campaigns — each shown only where the data exists, and placed where the context is.

Outcome
Shipped on direct demand from a top client and several teams — standing constraints set once, not re-keyed from a spreadsheet into every campaign.
Skill gained
Coordinating one feature across several teams building in parallel — running the shared refinements, keeping every squad clear on next steps.
04
Touchpoints · Data governance

See what a data update changes — before it changes.

Update icons littered the list; “apply all” had no preview, so you accepted blind. I replaced it with one banner and a single panel showing every change current vs. incoming, with a plain-language warning on risky ones (“this removes the RF curves on this audience”). Take everything, or pick parameter by parameter.

Outcome
A clean model engineering could rebuild on — shipped in about a month, early 2026, replacing a flow the team couldn’t safely maintain and users didn’t trust.
Skill gained
Working close to backend — mapping, with engineering, how data actually moves through the applications.
05
Audience Origin · Rebuild

Rebuilding the audience builder.

One of the platform’s most-used tools still ran on an old design system, adrift from everything around it, with navigation that was hard to follow. I rebuilt it on shared components and reworked the flow — a data-source tree, a canvas where AND/OR logic stays visible as you build, a live audience size, and audience creation as a short side panel.

Before After
Outcome
Shipped early 2025 to the platform’s largest user base — the most-used module of the set (exact figures confidential).
Skill gained
Data visualisation in depth — many chart types, and how each should behave when the layout flexes.

The through-line

None of these were greenfield. Each meant reading how data actually moves through the product, arguing a direction with Data Science or engineering, and shipping something a planner could trust.
